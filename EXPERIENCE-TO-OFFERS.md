# From professional experience to useful offers

This companion worksheet is for professionals who want to package experience from work they have actually done. It can be used alongside a salary or independent practice. The examples below are illustrative; they are not customer results or earnings claims.

## Start with a problem you have solved

Choose a recurring problem you understand from practice. Write down:

- Who had the problem, described without identifying a private client or employer.
- What they were trying to finish and where they got stuck.
- What you did, in what order, and why.
- The decisions a beginner would miss.
- The exceptions: when your approach would be unsuitable.
- What evidence supports the result, what remains uncertain, and what you may share.

Your professional title is context. The useful asset is a repeatable part of your experience that helps another person make progress. Use material you have permission to share; remove employer secrets and client information.

Illustrative starting points: a manager who has improved staff onboarding; a project coordinator who handles handoffs; a teacher who helps learners diagnose a recurring mistake; an independent practitioner who repeatedly explains the same preparation process. Each still needs to investigate who wants the result and what they already use.

## Extract a framework

Describe the decisions behind your work before naming the method. A framework should help its user decide what to do next.

1. Define the starting situation and the result the framework helps produce.
2. Identify the stages, decisions and evidence needed at each stage.
3. Show one worked example and one important exception.
4. Give the user a way to check their own work.
5. Test the explanation with someone who has the problem.

A memorable name can help communication. It does not establish that a method is effective or original. Do not invent research, qualifications, client outcomes or intellectual ownership of someone else's method.

## Choose an offer that fits the work

| Offer | What the buyer receives | What you need to validate |
|---|---|---|
| Workbook or checklist | A structured way to complete a defined task | Can the buyer use it without unexplained steps? |
| Guide or short course | Explanation, examples and practice | Does it teach the decisions needed for the promised task? |
| Workshop or coaching | Help applying the method to the buyer's situation | What preparation, scope and feedback can you responsibly provide? |
| Follow-up program | Continued help with a recurring need | Is that need real and recurring, and can you serve it reliably? |

Digital products may reduce repeated explanation. Coaching still uses your time. A higher price or an additional offer needs a useful difference in scope, depth, feedback or application; it does not create value by itself.

## Connect the offers through the next real problem

An illustrative journey is:

**A useful demonstration → a complete introductory product → guided application → an appropriate next step.**

The demonstration helps someone recognize whether the method fits. The first paid product must deliver its own promise. An optional coaching offer can help with application, judgment or feedback. A later offer should respond to a further problem the buyer actually has.

For each transition, record:

- What has the buyer already completed?
- What question or difficulty comes next?
- Can they continue without buying more? Explain how.
- What extra help would the next offer provide?
- What does it cost you to deliver that help?

This is an offer ladder and a simple funnel design, not a claim that every reader needs every offer. Do not make a purchased product incomplete to force an upsell. Do not require a mailing-list signup to obtain a resource already included with a purchase. Explain optional communications separately.

## Plan the economics and the limits

For each offer, estimate the amount received, fees, delivery costs, support, acquisition and expected refunds. Record the time you must personally provide. Separate one-time production from costs that repeat with each buyer.

Compare the work needed for a workbook with the work needed for a coaching session. Automated checkout and delivery do not make a live service passive. Keep the first delivery manageable, observe real questions and improve the offer before expanding it.

## Prompt for your AI assistant

Copy the following into an assistant appropriate for the information you intend to share. Begin with anonymized examples and avoid confidential material.

> Help me turn professional experience into a useful offer. Ask me about a problem I have actually solved, who needed help, the steps and decisions I used, evidence I can share and important exceptions. Do not invent expertise, results, testimonials, demand or earnings. Extract a possible framework from my answers. Suggest a workbook, guide or lesson that delivers one complete result. Then describe optional coaching or a further offer only if it solves a distinct next problem. Map a demonstration, the introductory product and those optional next steps. For each, identify what needs validation, the work I must do, the delivery process and the costs to record. Keep my claims within my actual experience and qualifications. Show what you inferred and what I still need to verify.

Use the other reader-kit templates to prepare the product brief, evidence ledger, offer and demonstration. The AI can help organize experience; it cannot supply a professional history you do not have.
