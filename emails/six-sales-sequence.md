# The Six-Sales Email Sequence (7 emails over 14 days)

An info-product sells six things, not one: (1) the decision they just made, (2) a first victory, (3) a first epiphany through a story, (4) continuing step by step, (5) integrating it into their life, (6) the next product. This is the author's adaptation of what he learned from Dan Kennedy's work.

Examples below use two fictional characters from the book: Frank, a master electrician, and Elena, a restaurant kitchen manager. Swap in your own field. Never invent results or testimonials; use only real ones with permission. No discounts, no countdown pressure.

---

## Email 1 (day 0): Sale 1, the decision
**Subject:** You made a good call

Hi [FIRST NAME],

Before anything else: asking for [PRODUCT] was a sound decision. It holds what took me [X years] to learn, and you can read it in [Y hours].

[One honest line on the value: for example, "A single mistake on a panel upgrade can cost a day's pay. This booklet is about not making that one."]

Start with page [X]. I will be here the whole way.

[NAME]

## Email 2 (day 1): Sale 2, the first victory
**Subject:** A ten-minute win

Hi [FIRST NAME],

Elena, who runs the line at a busy restaurant, says the first thing she teaches a new cook is one small job done perfectly. Not ten jobs. One.

Your one job today: [the first exercise]. Ten minutes. Reply and tell me what you wrote.

[NAME]

## Email 3 (day 3): Sale 3, the epiphany through a story
**Subject:** What Frank learned on a Tuesday

Hi [FIRST NAME],

Frank had wired houses for twenty years when an apprentice asked him a question he could not answer in one sentence: "How do you know it is safe?"

He realized he did it by five checks he had never written down. The day he wrote them down, he could teach them.

[Tell YOUR story in 6-8 lines: a moment when you saw your own knowledge for the first time.]

That is the idea at the center of [PRODUCT]: what you do without thinking can be taught.

[NAME]

## Email 4 (day 5): Sale 4, continue step by step
**Subject:** The next step (just one)

Hi [FIRST NAME],

Most people stop after the first win. That is normal. The next step is small: [step 2 of your product]. Here is how to do it in fifteen minutes: [3 lines].

Do it today, even badly. A rough version beats a perfect plan.

[NAME]

## Email 5 (day 8): Sale 5, integrate it
**Subject:** Make it part of your Tuesday

Hi [FIRST NAME],

Knowing is not the same as doing. The people who finish are the ones who give it a place in the week.

Pick a time. Elena does her planning Sunday night, after service, with a coffee. Put [PRODUCT] in your calendar: twenty minutes, twice a week, for two weeks.

Hit reply and tell me which slot you chose. Saying it out loud makes it real.

[NAME]

## Email 6 (day 11): Sale 5 again, answers and proof
**Subject:** The questions I get asked most

Hi [FIRST NAME],

Three questions I hear all the time:

1. "I am not an expert." You only need to be two steps ahead of the person you help.
2. "I do not have time." [Your answer with your product.]
3. "What if I get stuck?" Reply to this email.

[Optional: one real, permitted quote from a real buyer. If you have none yet, leave this out.]

[NAME]

## Email 7 (day 14): Sale 6, the next offer
**Subject:** If you want to go further

Hi [FIRST NAME],

You have had two weeks with [PRODUCT]. If it helped, there is a next level: [NEXT PRODUCT: what it is, who it is for, what they get, price, link].

It is a different, deeper product, not a discount on this one. If now is not the time, nothing changes: the booklet is yours.

Thank you for reading along.

[NAME]
